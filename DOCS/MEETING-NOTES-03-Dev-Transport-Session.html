03 What it changes in the spec

Three real model shifts.

Riders aren't just members

The membership gate stays — but guests ride too, mostly family and friends of members. The booking model needs a guest class alongside members, with its own registration and accountability rules.

A seat has a direction

Outbound and return are independent legs. A person can hold a return seat they never used outbound. Capacity, pricing and manifests all become per-leg, not per-trip.

The payment model simplifies

If "no cash" holds, the manual-payment path, receipt-proof question and cash-attestation audit machinery all shrink or disappear — digital settlement only, one source of truth.

⚠ "No cash" needs one confirmation: fully digital, or no cash handling but office transfers still recorded manually?
04 Questions to answer — guests

Guests are now a first-class entity.

Raised in session: two registration paths — public signup requiring admin approval, or link-based signup without approval — plus members onboarding guests directly.

  1. Can a guest book and pay for themselves, or do they always ride under the member who brought them?Decides whether guests get real accounts or exist as named riders on a member's booking
  2. Is a guest record one-off or permanent? If a visitor comes twice, do they re-register — and is there a guest → member conversion path?Most first-timers join through family and friends — conversion is the actual journey
  3. Guest caps? Max guests per member per programme — and a guest quota per bus?Prevents a nominally member-gated system becoming open public transport
  4. The link — shared or per-member? A per-member invite link shows who invited whom; a shared link is simpler but loses the referral chain.Determines accountability for who brought whom
  5. On the manifest — "Guest of [member]"? Should marshals see which member is responsible for each guest?
05 Questions to answer — trip legs & overflow

Outbound and return are different problems.

  1. Are outbound and return seats counted independently?A bus can be full going and have room returning — confirm that matches reality
  2. Does pricing differ by leg? Return-only passengers on a paid programme — full fare, half, free?
  3. Are legs chosen at booking or on the day? Can someone decide "I'm returning with the bus" without a prior booking — and can the marshal add them on the spot?
  4. Multiple departure times per leg? One return trip or several?
  5. When all buses fill — what happens? Extra bus called, people turned away, or priority order (members before guests, first-come)?The overfill answer decides whether a waitlist is in scope at all
06 Questions to answer — exceptions

Run this list past them: "have we missed any?"

  1. Passenger misses the outbound bus but wants to return on it — covered above, confirm the flow.
  2. Passenger returns on a different route than they booked outbound.
  3. Member brings an unregistered extra person on the day.
  4. Marshal doesn't show — who boards passengers?
  5. Bus breaks down mid-route or doesn't arrive at all.
  6. Person boards the wrong bus — discovered when?
  7. Which of these actually happen — and who is authorized to fix each one on the spot?
07 Questions to answer — gate, integration, refunds

The policy questions still open.

  1. "Admin approves first-timers" — what's the turnaround? If someone registers Friday night for Sunday, does a human realistically act in time?Queue vs. bottleneck — decides whether approval is a gate or a wall
  2. Phone verification (OTP) at signup — or is name + register match enough?Security vs. ease of use, stated priorities in tension
  3. Integration point — be specific. Does the member register live in the existing church SQL database? If yes: does this app read it directly, or receive periodic exports?Direct integration vs. copy-and-sync is a major architecture fork
  4. Who owns the existing PHP/SQL system and is it still maintained?
  5. Refund policy — who drafts it and by when? Give them the decision points to react to: cutoff timing · group partial refunds · return-leg refunds · what happens when the church cancels a bus.
  6. Stack decision timing — PHP/SQL exists today but wasn't assumed for this app. Confirm by when the stack call gets made — it blocks all schema and API work.
08 Finding the design smell

Things that look right but break in reality.

The exercise

Walk the prototype live — member and marshal screens on a phone. Each person points at anything that looks correct but would break in their real operation. That's how the cash-payment and guest gaps surfaced.

Smells to test against

Install gate before a guest can even see a seat? One payment covering legs people may not use? Approval queues stalling weekend registrations? A manifest that assumes everyone boards the same bus both ways?

The bar they set

Security — gated, attributed, correctable. Ease of use — bookable by someone who is not phone-savvy. Ease of management — operable without the person who built it.

Design smell = a decision made for convenience that fails under real conditions — catch them now, they're cheap
